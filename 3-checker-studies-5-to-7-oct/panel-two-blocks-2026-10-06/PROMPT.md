# A question for outside reviewers (6 October 2026)

An independent AI researcher in Canada works on receipts for AI agents. Every time an agent reports "done", it must point to a line in a record the agent cannot change, and every claim is answered "shown", "contradicted" or "not shown". He seals his study designs and predictions with public timestamps (FreeTSA, and Bitcoin through OpenTimestamps) before running them.

He calls the principle under his work "digital time travel": proving, at a later time and unchangeably, what was known at an earlier time. He sees two building blocks:

1. **A ladder that only grows (memory).** An append-only record in which every entry pairs two separate things, for example a claim and the evidence for it, or two halves of a secret. New rungs are added; old ones never change; it never closes.
2. **A Mobius-strip loop (the check).** Something is sealed (fixed and hidden) and later revealed. The loop returns to its start "seen from the other side": what was committed can be checked against what happened, and neither can be changed afterwards.

He uses them now for AI agents' "done" claims, sealed predictions and timestamped findings. He believes the same two blocks have many other uses.

Please answer, using the web to check what exists:

1. **Other uses.** List the most valuable uses outside his current one, ranked, each in two or three sentences: who needs it, what it would prove, and what it would replace.
2. **What already exists.** Name the existing technologies, standards and research these resemble, with links (for example append-only and transparency logs, commit-reveal schemes, trusted timestamping, secret sharing, pre-registration). Then say plainly what, if anything, is distinct about using the two together as described, and what is not.
3. **Failure modes.** How these break or get gamed: privacy, a sealed claim that is false (sealed is not true), who controls the record, lost keys, people misreading what a timestamp proves.
4. **How to say it.** One paragraph he could use publicly that is precise and does not overclaim.
5. **One experiment.** The single experiment that would best test the most valuable new use, sealed in advance: what is measured, and what result would show it doesn't work.

Rules: don't post, publish, sign up or contact anyone. Cite sources with links. Mark anything you're unsure about as unsure.
