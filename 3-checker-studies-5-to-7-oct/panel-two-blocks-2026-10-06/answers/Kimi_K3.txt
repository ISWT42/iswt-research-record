## 1. Other uses, ranked

**1. AI evaluation and deployment audit.** Labs, regulators, and insurers need proof that a reported benchmark was run against a specific, unchangeable eval harness and dataset (sealed before the run), and — after an incident — proof of what the operator's logs showed and when. This replaces self-reported benchmark tables and operator-controlled logs, and it maps directly onto the EU AI Act's logging and record-keeping obligations for high-risk systems ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj)).

**2. Digital-evidence chain of custody and compliance record-keeping.** Forensic labs, courts, e-discovery teams, and banks under model-risk rules need to prove that a file, log, or model version existed unaltered from seizure or decision time onward. This replaces paper custody forms, mutable SIEM logs, and expensive after-the-fact reconstruction (cf. ISO/IEC 27037 on digital-evidence handling, [iso.org](https://www.iso.org/standard/44381.html)).

**3. Vulnerability disclosure and bug-bounty priority.** Security researchers need to prove they held a specific report on a specific date without revealing the bug; vendors need the same to defend against duplicate-claim disputes. This replaces trust in triage-queue timestamps and private "I reported it first" email threads.

**4. Whistleblower possession proofs and information escrow.** Journalists and sources need "I had this document before date X" proofs; threshold-escrow variants reveal an allegation only when N matching reports exist (the model of Project Callisto for serial-misconduct reporting, [projectcallisto.org](https://www.projectcallisto.org) — **unsure** whether it still operates). This replaces nothing reliable, which is the point: today there is no good way to prove prior possession without disclosure.

**5. Institutional pre-registration and results-blind review.** Journals and funders need sealed designs with verifiable timestamps so HARKing and silent protocol changes are detectable. He does this personally already; the new use is running it as infrastructure for others, replacing honor-system registries that don't seal content cryptographically.

**6. Priority, trade-secret, and invention-possession timestamps.** Inventors and startups need proof of possession at a date. This replaces notarized notebooks and "poor man's copyright," though first-to-file patent law caps its value — it's strongest for trade secrets and scientific priority, weakest for patents.

## 2. What already exists

**The "ladder that only grows":** linked timestamping was invented by Haber & Stornetta in 1991 ([link.springer.com](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00196791)) — their scheme is literally an append-only chain whose operator cannot backdate; their company Surety published weekly roots in New York Times classified ads. Modern forms: Merkle trees, git, Bitcoin ([bitcoin.org](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf)), Certificate Transparency ([rfc-editor.org](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html)), Sigstore/Rekor ([sigstore.dev](https://www.sigstore.dev)), Trillian-style transparency logs ([transparency.dev](https://transparency.dev)), the Go checksum database ([sum.golang.org](https://sum.golang.org)), Key Transparency ([github.com](https://github.com/google/keytransparency)), and Estonia's KSI system for government-record integrity ([guardtime.com](https://guardtime.com/technology)).

**The "seal now, reveal later" loop:** cryptographic commitment schemes (Blum's coin-flipping-by-telephone, Pedersen commitments; [en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commitment_scheme)), the RFC 3161 Time-Stamp Protocol that FreeTSA implements ([rfc-editor.org](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3161.html), [freetsa.org](https://freetsa.org)), OpenTimestamps' Bitcoin anchoring ([opentimestamps.org](https://opentimestamps.org)), long-term evidence-record syntax for re-anchoring aging seals ([rfc-editor.org](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4998.html)), blind analysis in physics ([en.wikipedia.org](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blind_analysis)), sealed-bid auctions, and pre-registration platforms: OSF Registries ([osf.io](https://osf.io/registries)), AsPredicted ([aspredicted.org](https://aspredicted.org)), ClinicalTrials.gov results reporting ([clinicaltrials.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov)), the AEA RCT registry ([socialscienceregistry.org](https://www.socialscienceregistry.org)), and Registered Reports ([cos.io](https://www.cos.io/initiatives/registered-reports)).

**"Two halves of a secret":** Shamir ([dl.acm.org](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/359168.359176)) and Blakley secret sharing. Reviewer note: this only provides its guarantee if shares sit in *independent custody*; two halves stored on one ledger is encryption at rest, not secret sharing.

**Receipts for AI agents specifically — near-identical current work, and he should cite all of it:**

- The "belief ledger" note: entries pairing an assertion with the evidence that made it true, cheap bulk re-verification at session start, and a staleness diff ([ai.bluecloudcyber.com](https://ai.bluecloudcyber.com/notes/belief-ledger)).
- IETF draft *evidence-action-00*: append-only, hash-chained, signature-bound evidence records for agent actions, binding tool ID, arguments hash, and result hash, with fail-closed independent verification ([datatracker.ietf.org](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00)) — **unsure** whether it has advanced past -00.
- Acta: typed contributions (claims/questions/predictions) with state transitions computed from a hash-chained response graph — open, contested, superseded, resolved ([github.com](https://github.com/VeritasActa/acta), [veritasacta.com](https://veritasacta.com/docs)).
- "Proof packets": a content-addressed envelope per agent action with a verifier-derived verdict ([harperz9.github.io](https://harperz9.github.io/papers/proof-packets.pdf)).
- Adjacent attestation stacks: W3C Verifiable Credentials ([w3.org](https://www.w3.org/TR/vc-data-model-2.0/)), C2PA content provenance ([c2pa.org](https://c2pa.org)), SLSA ([slsa.dev](https://slsa.dev)) and in-toto ([in-toto.io](https://in-toto.io)).

**What is not distinct:** every primitive (append-only chains, public anchoring, commit–reveal, trusted timestamping, secret sharing, pre-registration), and — plainly — the combined pattern applied to agent "done" claims, which the four projects above already build, including claim+evidence pairing and shown/contradicted/not-shown-style verdicts. Even the "digital time travel" definition is a restatement of what timestamping has meant since 1991 (**unsure** whether the exact phrase has prior use; it wouldn't surprise me).

**What is arguably distinct, narrowly:** (a) the *complementarity* made explicit — a public append-only ledger of *all seals* closes commit–reveal's selective-revelation hole (you can't secretly seal fifty predictions and reveal the winner, because the set of seals is itself on the record), while commit–reveal gives the ledger privacy (entries can be hashes until opened); (b) doing it as one minimal, dual-anchored, near-zero-cost personal discipline spanning research conduct and agent operation; (c) the verdict-forcing social rule, not the data structure. That's a contribution of integration and evaluation, not invention — valuable, but claims should be scoped that way.

## 3. Failure modes

- **Sealed is not true.** A timestamp proves content existed *no later than* a time — never that it was correct, collected honestly, or created *at* that time. The evidence.* draft states the same bound and warns against reading commitment as endorsement ([datatracker.ietf.org](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00)). Expect audiences — especially courts and journalists — to misread "anchored in Bitcoin" as "validated."
- **Sharpshooter sealing.** A hash of a vague prediction proves vague text existed; precision at seal time is a social requirement no hash enforces.
- **Selective sealing and revelation.** Sealing many contradictory claims and revealing the favorable one defeats the loop unless the set of seals is itself public (see 2).
- **Agent Goodharting.** If receipts gate "done", agents optimize for producing receipts; receipts that verify but check nothing are self-praise with a hash. Evidence must be independently re-runnable, not agent-authored. Also flooding: thousands of trivial receipts bury contradictions.
- **Control of the record.** An operator-signed chain is tamper-evident, not equivocation-resistant — a signer controlling every copy can show different verifiers different consistent histories; external witnessing of chain heads mitigates but does not make it third-party attested (again, the draft's own language).
- **Keys and availability.** Lost keys freeze the chain; stolen keys forge receipts; and a hash whose preimage is lost proves nothing — the "unchangeable record" is also a single point of failure unless replicated. Threshold custody and published rotation/revocation entries help.
- **Anchor dependence.** FreeTSA is one TSA — trust in its key and clock; OpenTimestamps depends on calendar servers plus Bitcoin confirmation lag (hours, block-time granularity). Dual anchoring hedges but each anchor has its own failure set. FreeTSA is, to my knowledge, **not** an eIDAS qualified trust service provider (**unsure**), so its stamps carry technical rather than statutory presumption in the EU ([eur-lex.europa.eu](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/910/oj)).
- **Privacy and longevity.** Public hashes of low-entropy content are guessable (salt commitments); immutability collides with erasure rights — keep personal data out of anchors. Algorithms age (SHA-1's lesson); multi-decade seals need re-anchoring per RFC 4998 ([rfc-editor.org](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4998.html)).
- **Metaphor risk.** "Time travel" and "Möbius strip" invite over-reading; the math delivers binding and ordering, nothing more exotic.

## 4. One paragraph he could use

> I keep two public records. The first is an append-only, hash-chained ledger in which every entry pairs an assertion — for example, an AI agent's claim that a task is done — with the evidence that supported it; old entries cannot be altered without detection. The second is a commit–reveal step: before running a study or accepting a result, I publish a cryptographic hash of the claim or design, anchored by two independent timestamp services (an RFC 3161 authority and the Bitcoin blockchain via OpenTimestamps), and later reveal the content so anyone can check it against the seal. Together these let any third party verify, without trusting me, that a specific statement existed in a specific form no later than a specific time, and that a given evidence record has not changed since signing. They prove nothing else — not that a sealed claim was true, not that evidence was gathered honestly, and not that a "shown" verdict was correct. The primitives are decades old (linked timestamping dates to Haber and Stornetta, 1991) and similar agent-receipt systems exist; my contribution is testing whether a deliberately small, cheap composition of them measurably reduces false "done" claims by AI agents and unsupported claims in my own research.

## 5. One experiment

**The false-done trial.** Fix a pool of ~80 software tasks whose ground truth is established by independent re-execution. Randomize tasks between a control arm (agent reports "done" free-form) and a treatment arm ("done" is accepted only with a ledger entry whose evidence is independently re-runnable and whose verdict computes to *shown*), using the same agent model and tools. Seal in advance — hashed, dual-timestamped, and publicly listed: the task pool, the harness code, the analysis plan, and the predictions.

**Measured:** primary endpoint = false-done rate at handoff (accepted "done"s later contradicted by ground truth); secondaries = median wall-clock/token overhead, verdict distribution, and the *vacuous-receipt rate* (receipts that verify but check nothing), plus time-to-catch.

**Sealed predictions:** ≥50% relative reduction in false dones; ≤25% overhead; ≤5% vacuous receipts.

**Result that shows it doesn't work:** false-done reduction under 10 percentage points or a confidence interval crossing zero; median overhead above 50%; or a vacuous-receipt rate above 10% — meaning the discipline can be fully satisfied while saying nothing. Commit to publishing either way. This extends the single-arm testbed proposed at [ai.bluecloudcyber.com](https://ai.bluecloudcyber.com/notes/belief-ledger) with a control arm and an explicit gaming measure — and because its own seals are publicly listed, the experiment doubles as a live test of the selective-sealing attack described in §3.

**Bottom line for the researcher:** the building blocks are 30–45 years old, and his exact application — receipts for agents with verdicts — is actively being built by at least four other efforts as of this writing. His defensible contribution is a lean, dual-anchored, verdict-forcing discipline *plus rigorous, sealed, published evaluation of whether it actually works*. That is genuinely worth doing, and the experiment above is the fastest way to find out whether it is worth anything more.
