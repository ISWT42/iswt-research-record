
### 1. Other Uses (Ranked by Value)  
1. **Scientific Research Pre-registration**  
   *Who needs it:* Academic researchers and journals.  
   *What it proves:* Timestamped pre-registration of hypotheses/methods prevents p-hacking and confirms unbiased results.  
   *Replaces:* Manual pre-registration platforms (e.g., OSF) that lack cryptographic integrity checks.  

2. **Legal Contract Execution**  
   *Who needs it:* Legal firms and smart-contract platforms.  
   *What it proves:* Immutable proof of agreement terms and fulfillment (e.g., escrow releases upon verified conditions).  
   *Replaces:* Traditional notarization and centralized blockchain oracles.  

3. **Supply Chain Auditing**  
   *Who needs it:* Regulators (e.g., FDA), ethical consumers.  
   *What it proves:* Authenticity of product journeys (e.g., organic/fair-trade claims) via sealed evidence at each step.  
   *Replaces:* Paper trails and easily falsified digital logs.  

4. **Voting Systems**  
   *Who needs it:* Election commissions.  
   *What it proves:* Ballot integrity without compromising anonymity, using sealed votes later verified against tallies.  
   *Replaces:* Opaque e-voting machines and manual recounts.  

5. **Intellectual Property Timestamping**  
   *Who needs it:* Inventors, artists.  
   *What it proves:* Ownership priority (e.g., patent/copyright) via unchangeable "first-to-file" evidence.  
   *Replaces:* "Poor man’s copyright" (self-mailing) or commercial timestamping services.

### 2. What Already Exists  
**Technologies/Standards**:  
- **Append-Only Logs**: Transparency logs (e.g., [Certificate Transparency](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/rfc9162)), blockchain ledgers.  
- **Commit-Reveal Schemes**: Cryptographic commitments (e.g., hash-based seals in voting/auctions).  
- **Trusted Timestamping**: RFC 3161 (TSA), Bitcoin anchors ([OpenTimestamps](https://opentimestamps.org)).  
- **AI Evidence Ledgers**: [Belief Ledgers](https://ai.bluecloudcyber.com/notes/belief-ledger) (stale-proof assertions), [Acta protocol](https://github.com/VeritasActa/acta) (contestable claims).  
- **Action Provenance**: [IETF evidence.* family](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00) (tamper-evident action logs), [Proof Packets](https://harperz9.github.io/papers/proof-packets.pdf).  

**Distinctive vs. Existing Work**:  
- ✅ **Novelty**: Combining the "ladder" (append-only evidence pairs) with "Mobius loop" (commit-reveal) creates a *unified system for incremental verification*. This enables efficient "diffs" of stale claims (e.g., Belief Ledger’s session-start checks) and contextual reveals (e.g., sealed predictions). Existing tools handle these separately.  
- ❌ **Not New**: Timestamping, append-only logs, or commit schemes alone are well-established. The AI-specific framing (e.g., agent "done" receipts) builds on [Acta](https://veritasacta.com/docs) and [IETF evidence.*](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00).

### 3. Failure Modes  
- **False Sealed Claims**: Sealing proves *existence* at a time, not truth (e.g., an agent could seal fabricated data). Verifiers may conflate timestamped existence with validity. [IETF draft §8](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00) emphasizes this risk.  
- **Privacy Leaks**: Public ladders could expose sensitive data (e.g., research hypotheses). Zero-knowledge proofs may be needed.  
- **Centralized Control**: If the ledger is operator-controlled (e.g., single entity), equivocation attacks are possible. [IETF §9.2](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/html/draft-msebenzi-evidence-action-00) notes this requires external witnessing.  
- **Lost Keys**: Irrecoverable if signing keys are lost, rendering records unverifiable.  
- **Misread Timestamps**: Users might assume timestamps prove *correctness* (they only prove *antiquity*).

### 4. Public Description (Precise & Non-Overclaiming)  
> "This work enables 'digital time travel' via two mechanisms: (1) an append-only ledger pairing claims with their supporting evidence, and (2) a commit-reveal loop that seals data for later verification. Together, they let anyone prove—at a future time—what was known or asserted earlier, with cryptographic assurance against alteration. While building on transparency logs and timestamping, our integration uniquely supports efficient re-verification for dynamic AI agents. It does not guarantee truthfulness, only immutable record-keeping and temporal anchoring."

### 5. One Experiment  
**Test**: Apply the system to **scientific pre-registration**.  
**Sealed in Advance**: Hypothesis, methodology, and analysis plan timestamped via OpenTimestamps/Bitcoin.  
**Measure**:  
- Cost/time to verify ledger entries vs. manual audits.  
- Discrepancy rate between pre-registered plans and published results.  
- Auditor success in detecting tampered evidence (e.g., altered statistical methods).  
**Failure Condition**: If >10% of tampered records go undetected by auditors, or verification costs exceed traditional methods by >50%, the system fails practicality.  

**Uncertainties**: Scalability for large-scale studies; ethical constraints on real-world tampering tests.
