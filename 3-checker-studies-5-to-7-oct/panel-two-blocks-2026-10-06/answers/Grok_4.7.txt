## 1. Other uses

Ranked by who is harmed when the producer can omit or rewrite, and by how much the pair of blocks does that a timestamp alone does not. His current uses — agent “done” claims, sealed predictions, timestamped findings — are left out.

**1. Complete reporting of pre-specified outcomes.** Trial sponsors, journals, regulators, and systematic reviewers need this. It would prove that the outcome set and analysis rule were fixed before results existed, and that a later paper is the committed set or a visible departure from it. It would replace “we registered on ClinicalTrials.gov,” which makes switching detectable only if someone compares the registry with the paper. That comparison is often not done, and silent switching remains common. [[1]](https://casrai.org/guides/outcome-switching-and-selective-outcome-reporting) [[2]](https://www.transparimed.org/single-post/outcome-switching-research-misconduct) [[3]](https://www.bmj.com/content/393/bmj-2025-087975)

**2. Sealed public procurement.** Buying agencies and bidders need this. It would prove each bid was committed before any bid was opened, that the opened bid matches that commitment, and that the agency did not drop or insert a bid after seeing the others. It would replace sealed envelopes and trust in the auctioneer. Commit-reveal already does the hiding; the append-only log is what makes omission visible. [[4]](https://tokentoolhub.com/commit-reveal-schemes/) [[5]](https://blog.shutter.network/tired-of-getting-sniped-send-your-dapps-this-commit-reveal-fix/)

**3. Software, firmware, and model release logs.** Users and maintainers need this wherever a download page is the only record. It would prove that the digest everyone can fetch is the digest that was published, and that the publisher cannot show one release to a victim and another to everyone else. It would replace “trust the vendor site.” Much of this already runs for certificates, Go modules, some phone images, and Sigstore signatures; the open gap is places that still publish a file and a claim with no witnessed log, including many model-weight releases. [[6]](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Security/Defenses/Certificate_Transparency) [[7]](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/sigstore-rekor-transparency-log-deep-dive-2026) [[8]](https://transparency.dev/articles/tile-based-logs/)

**4. Key and account directories.** Users of encrypted messaging need this. It would prove that the key shown to you is the key shown to everyone else, and that a change is an appended entry someone can monitor. It would replace trust-on-first-use and the provider’s private database. This is deployed, not hypothetical: CONIKS-style designs are in production at WhatsApp, iMessage, and Proton. [[9]](https://eprint.iacr.org/archive/2014/1004/1428180619.pdf) [[10]](https://eprint.iacr.org/2023/1515.pdf)

**5. Public records, newsroom files, and election materials.** Journalists, observers, and courts need this when a file may be edited or a production may be incomplete. It would prove a byte string existed by a witnessed time, and, if an inventory was logged, that a later bundle is complete relative to that inventory. It would replace platform clocks and “the officer says this is the file.” Election-document timestamping with OpenTimestamps has already been used this way; a single stamp still does not prove the set was complete. [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenTimestamps) [[12]](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m4zDethg5e4)

**6. Liability snapshots and claim files.** Depositors, auditors, and insurers need this. It would prove a stated set of obligations existed at a pinned checkpoint, and that a claim stays bound to the evidence sealed with it rather than to evidence chosen later. It would replace a point-in-time letter and editable file metadata. A reserves selfie that omits liabilities is not this.

Lower, and easier to fake with one stamp: whistleblower dossiers opened after a story breaks, and insurance photos sealed before adjustment. Those need the reveal. They need the growing ladder only if the file itself grows and omissions matter.

## 2. What already exists

**Append-only, checkable memory.** Haber and Stornetta (1991) chained document hashes so a stamp could not be inserted or deleted even with a colluding timestamp service, and published the chain so others could check it. [[13]](https://web.archive.org/web/20180411111447im_/https:/page-one.live.cf.public.springer.com/pdf/preview/10.1007/BF00196791) [[14]](https://decentralizedthoughts.github.io/2020-07-05-the-first-blockchain-or-how-to-time-stamp-a-digital-document/) Certificate Transparency logs certificates in append-only Merkle trees; RFC 9162 is the current spec. A signed certificate timestamp is a promise of later inclusion, checked afterward against the tree. [[15]](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com) [[16]](https://research.swtch.com/tlog) The same shape is in Trillian and Tessera, the Go checksum database, Pixel and firmware binary transparency, and Sigstore Rekor. [[17]](https://google.github.io/trillian/) [[8]](https://transparency.dev/articles/tile-based-logs/) [[7]](https://safeguard.sh/resources/blog/sigstore-rekor-transparency-log-deep-dive-2026) SCITT (RFC 9943, June 2026) is the supply-chain version: a transparency service appends signed statements and returns a receipt. The registration time is the service’s own clock unless something else anchors it. [[18]](https://www.ietf.org/ietf-ftp/rfc/rfc9943.html)

**The seal-and-later-open.** That is a commitment scheme: hide a value, bind yourself to it, open it later. Sealed-bid auctions, commit-reveal voting, and commit-reveal prediction markets are the deployed forms. [[4]](https://tokentoolhub.com/commit-reveal-schemes/) [[19]](https://docs.predictionarenas.app/how-it-works/commit-reveal) Scientific pre-registration is the same loop without the cryptography: OSF freezes a timestamped snapshot; ClinicalTrials.gov and CONSORT are the clinical versions. Registration makes a switch detectable. It does not make the registered claim true, and it does not force anyone to read the registry. [[20]](https://casrai.org/guides/osf-preregistration) [[21]](https://casrai.org/dictionary/term/pre-registration) [[22]](https://www.bmj.com/content/356/bmj.j396)

**Time anchors.** RFC 3161 is the TSA protocol. Its own statement of purpose is evidence that a datum existed before a time, not that an event happened at that time. [[23]](https://www.iankduncan.com/projects/rfc-browser/3161) FreeTSA is a public RFC 3161 service; trust is in its key and clock. [[24]](https://freetsa.org/) OpenTimestamps commits a hash into Bitcoin through aggregating calendars and returns a portable proof. A timestamp there is proof the data existed prior to a block, not authorship or ownership. [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenTimestamps) [[25]](https://gwern.net/doc/www/petertodd.org/8acad037d1201985b33477a574c506e74c7d9074.html) An individual draft, draft-fassbender-scitt-time-anchor, specifies anchoring SCITT artifacts with OpenTimestamps. It is not an RFC. [[26]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-fassbender-scitt-time-anchor-07.html)

**Pairing two things.** Hashing a pair, or storing a typed link from a claim record to an evidence record, is binding, not a new primitive. Secret sharing (Shamir and later schemes) is different: shares reconstruct a secret and are meant to reveal nothing alone. Putting two shares on one rung is a storage choice, not the check. I did not find a standard whose point is “every rung is a pair, and the log never closes” as a named construction. Unsure whether any paper uses that exact ladder rule.

**Closest public cousin of the agent use.** Signed, hash-chained agent receipts already exist as a W3C-VC-style spec, an IETF individual draft, and running proxies. [[27]](https://agentreceipts.ai/specification/overview/) [[28]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-sahu-agent-action-receipts-00.html) Steven Mih’s individual drafts (Evidence Layer, evidence-request interaction, Checkpointed Local Log; Action State Group, October 2026) say a receipt proves inclusion and not truth, separate a store’s commit time from a claimed event time, and state that a self-consistent append-only history is possible until an outside party pins a checkpoint. [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html) [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html) Those drafts are work in progress, not standards. I am not asserting they are his; they are the closest public match I found to “shown / contradicted / not shown” discipline. Identity is unsure.

**What is distinct, and what is not.** Not distinct: append-only Merkle logs, commit-reveal, RFC 3161, Bitcoin timestamping, pre-registration, or the slogan that a seal is not truth. The Möbius-strip image is a teaching picture of commit-then-open. It is not a separate mechanism. What can be distinct in practice, if he actually enforces it, is the joint rule: a claim is admissible only as a pointer into a log the speaker cannot rewrite; claim and evidence are one committed pair, so evidence cannot be swapped onto an old claim; the only answers are shown, contradicted, or not shown; and a checkpoint is pinned outside the speaker before the outcome exists. That joint rule is a discipline on top of existing parts. It is not a new cryptographic object. Several of those limits are already written down in RFC 9943 and in the evidence-layer draft. [[18]](https://www.ietf.org/ietf-ftp/rfc/rfc9943.html) [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html)

## 3. Failure modes

**Sealed is not true.** A valid FreeTSA token or OpenTimestamps proof shows that a hash existed by a time. It does not show that the sentence inside was right, that the named person wrote it, or that the event happened when a clock field says. [[23]](https://www.iankduncan.com/projects/rfc-browser/3161) [[11]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenTimestamps) [[30]](https://www.rightsdocket.com/insights/rfc-3161-timestamps-music-creators) A false claim, sealed early, verifies perfectly.

**Cherry-picking.** Stamp many predictions, or many analyses, and open only the flattering ones. RFC 3161 by itself cannot show the missing ones. [[31]](https://www.vbase.com/blog/beyond-rfc-3161/) The ladder closes this only if the full set was committed before outcomes, and some party who is not the speaker kept that checkpoint. A private log the speaker controls does not.

**Who holds the record.** If the same party appends and is the only witness, they can present a byte-for-byte append-only history that never happened, then pin it. Internal consistency proves they did not rewrite a checkpoint an outsider already holds. It proves nothing about a checkpoint no outsider has seen. [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html) Split views are the same bug Certificate Transparency still treats as a monitoring problem; RFC 9162 left gossip unspecified. [[15]](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9162.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com) An agent that writes its own “done” line has not met “a record the agent cannot change” unless that line was already in a log the agent does not operate, or a witness pinned it before the agent could benefit from a rewrite.

**What the time actually is.** RFC 3161 is “existed before,” and it moves trust to the TSA. A stolen TSA key can forge times inside the certificate’s life; after a compromise, old tokens from that key become hard to trust. [[31]](https://www.vbase.com/blog/beyond-rfc-3161/) [[32]](https://havenmessenger.com/blog/posts/trusted-timestamping-rfc3161-explained/) Bitcoin anchoring is an upper bound after the proof is complete, not a precise second. Pending OpenTimestamps proofs are not yet that bound. Block timestamps are miner clocks; under adversarial conditions their accuracy is poor. I am unsure of the exact skew a verifier should allow; do not treat a block time as a lab clock. Registration order in a transparency service is not event order unless the policy says so. [[18]](https://www.ietf.org/ietf-ftp/rfc/rfc9943.html) [[33]](https://btw.media/zh/shouju-xieru-zhangben-weidai-jueiding-xinren-rfc9943)

**Lost keys and lost openings.** If the salt or preimage is lost, the commitment cannot be opened. The hash still existed; what it meant does not. If the `.tsr` or `.ots` file is lost, the time proof cannot be checked. A signature proves key control at signing, not role or authority. [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html)

**Privacy.** Only the hash should leave the machine, which hides the file if the file cannot be guessed. Low-entropy claims can be confirmed by recomputing the hash. Inclusion-proof siblings leak the same way unless each record carries fresh randomness. [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html) Stamping several files together, or stamping often, links them by time. [[34]](https://github.com/opentimestamps/opentimestamps-client) A checkpoint’s size leaks how much was recorded, not only what was shared with one reader. [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html) Append-only fights deletion. A legal right to erase is a real conflict, not a footnote. I am unsure how he intends to handle that.

**Misreading the three answers.** “Not shown” is not “false.” “Contradicted” needs a committed counter-record, not a missing row. A spoken “we have nothing” is not a checked absence. The evidence-layer draft separates those, and says a fabricated “no evidence” is easy if non-membership is not checkable against a pinned checkpoint. [[29]](https://www.ietf.org/archive/id/draft-mih-agent-evidence-layer-00.html)

**Legal overread.** I am unsure of the weight a Canadian court would give a FreeTSA token or an OpenTimestamps proof. Technical verification is not a finding that the underlying event occurred. Do not say the seal makes a record admissible.

## 4. How to say it

Digital time travel, as used here, means a later check can show that a specific byte string was fixed before that check, and that a witnessed log was not rewritten after an outside party pinned it. A claim is entered as a pair with the evidence offered for it, in a log that only grows. The claim is sealed, then opened against what happened. The only answers are shown, contradicted, or not shown. A FreeTSA token and an OpenTimestamps proof can show that a hash existed by a time. They do not show that the text was true, who wrote it, that the set was complete, or that an event occurred at the printed minute. A log the speaker alone can checkpoint does not bind the speaker. The method replaces “trust the later story” with “check this commitment against this record.” It does not replace judgment about whether the evidence was enough.

## 5. One experiment

Seal this before any outcomes exist. Call it a completeness test, not a truth test.

Build a batch of synthetic studies whose results a third party generates after the seal, from a public process the operator does not control. Before that generation, the operator must commit the full outcome list and the analysis rule. Three arms, assigned in advance: A, each claim timestamped alone, no committed set; B, an append-only log whose only checkpoints the operator holds; C, the same log, with an independent monitor pinning the pre-outcome checkpoint and anchoring that pin with FreeTSA and OpenTimestamps. The operator is scored for publishing a favorable story and may omit, relabel, or swap evidence. Blind reviewers, and a dumb checker that only verifies proofs, label each pre-specified claim shown, contradicted, or not shown, and label the bundle complete or not.

It does not work if, against the margin written in the sealed plan, arm C does not reduce accepted incomplete bundles relative to both A and B; or if a second log that verifies internally but was never pinned is accepted as often as the pinned one; or if sealed false claims are marked shown rather than merely committed. The last failure means the vocabulary collapsed into “sealed, therefore true,” which no amount of hashing fixes.
